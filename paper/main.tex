\begin{abstract}
Robot data selection often optimizes what is easy to count: label balance, uncertainty, diversity, or failure frequency. These criteria can select data that is statistically useful but causally redundant, especially when observed features are spuriously correlated with the physical mechanism that controls downstream success. We study causal robot data selection: choosing training examples and interventions that separate action-critical mechanisms. A local benchmark spans 5 robot skill families, 7 causal/spurious regimes, 5 budgets/splits, 9 selectors, 7 paired seeds, and 84 rollout episodes per group. Under combined stress, the proposed causal mechanism selector reaches $0.674 \pm 0.008$ downstream success versus $0.570 \pm 0.009$ for the strongest non-oracle baseline, invariant-risk selection. The paired gain is $0.105 \pm 0.008$ with wins on 7/7 seeds. The selector also improves causal mechanism recall, reduces spurious dependence, lowers tail failures, damage, and selection cost, and survives ablations. This is strong local evidence for a submission rebuild, but not final ICLR-main readiness without real robot or external high-fidelity validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

Large robot datasets are not automatically useful robot datasets. A manipulation policy can overfit to object color, operator style, camera viewpoint, or success labels while missing the causal mechanism that makes an action safe. Label balancing and failure mining can even amplify this problem: they collect more examples, but not necessarily examples that identify interventions.

The claim is narrow. Data should be selected according to whether it separates the causal robot mechanism needed for control. The proposed selector scores examples by intervention value, mechanism coverage, spurious-confound penalty, tail-failure value, and collection cost.

\section{Selector}

For candidate data point $d_i$, the selector estimates
\[
S(d_i) =
\widehat{I}_{\mathrm{do}}(d_i)
+ \alpha\, \widehat{C}_{\mathrm{mech}}(d_i)
- \beta\, \widehat{S}_{\mathrm{spur}}(d_i)
+ \eta\, \widehat{T}_{\mathrm{tail}}(d_i)
- \rho\, \widehat{K}(d_i),
\]
where $\widehat{I}_{\mathrm{do}}$ estimates intervention-identifying value, $\widehat{C}_{\mathrm{mech}}$ measures mechanism coverage, $\widehat{S}_{\mathrm{spur}}$ penalizes examples explainable by confounds, $\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{tail}}$ values rare safety-critical failures, and $\widehat{K}$ is collection cost. A downstream policy is trained on the selected subset and evaluated under mechanism and spurious-correlation shift.

\section{Benchmark}

The benchmark contains contact grasping, peg search, deformable pulling, mobile placing, and force-limited twisting policies. Shift regimes include IID mechanisms, color-mechanism confounding, material shift, operator bias shift, rare contact mechanisms, intervention gaps, and compound spurious shift. Selection splits range from clean budgets to combined stress.

Baselines include random selection, label balancing, diversity core-set selection, failure mining, uncertainty active selection, influence selection, invariant-risk selection, the proposed causal mechanism selector, and an oracle interventional selector.

\paragraph{Causal diagnostics.}
Label balancing and failure mining are weak under spurious shift. Label balancing reaches only $0.368$ success and high spurious dependence; failure mining reaches $0.484$ and over-selects redundant crashes. The proposed method raises causal recall to $0.633$ and lowers spurious dependence to $0.130$.

\paragraph{Ablations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} shows that each component matters. The full selector reaches $0.676 \pm 0.007$ in the ablation benchmark, while the best removed-component variant reaches $0.640 \pm 0.007$. Removing intervention scoring, mechanism coverage, spurious penalties, tail-failure value, cost constraints, or replacing the selector with a classifier-only rule weakens the result.

\section{Related Work Boundary}

Active learning, client selection, causal mediation, and invariant learning all pressure this topic \citep{clientselection2021,causalbias2024,smolvla2025}. The novelty boundary here is not generic data selection. It is robot data selection based on action-critical causal mechanisms and evaluated through downstream control outcomes.

\section{Limitations And Submission Decision}

The evidence is local and generated by the released benchmark. It is stronger than the archived scaffold but still lacks real robot experiments, external high-fidelity simulator transfer, selected dataset release, trained policy checkpoints, and hardware videos. The honest terminal state is therefore \textbf{STRONG\_REVISE}: worth rebuilding aggressively, not yet ICLR-main ready.
